01

The question

Knowing how many customers churned each period is table stakes. The more useful question is which customers are most likely to churn, and which specific factors are actually driving that risk, so retention effort can be targeted at the right accounts instead of applied as a blanket discount to everyone.

02

The approach

Working from a telecom customer dataset covering contract type, tenure, monthly charges, service add-ons and final status (stayed vs. churned), I cleaned and prepared the data in Python, converting categorical service fields into a modelling-ready format and engineering a customer value score from total revenue.

I then fit a logistic regression against churn status to isolate which factors carry real predictive weight, rather than relying on intuition about what "should" matter. The strongest drivers that came out of the model:

  • Month-to-month contracts, by a wide margin over annual contracts
  • Absence of online security or premium tech support add-ons
  • Fiber optic internet customers, who churned at a higher rate than DSL or cable
  • Customers with zero referrals, a weak but consistent signal

Key finding

Contract type outweighs every other factor: month-to-month customers churn at a far higher rate than annual subscribers, regardless of how much they spend.

Practically, this points toward incentivising longer contract terms, rather than discounting price alone, as the highest-leverage retention lever available.
